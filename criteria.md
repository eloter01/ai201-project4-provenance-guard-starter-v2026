# Acceptance criteria — Provenance Guard

Five criteria that say what "working" means for this service, written in unit 7
**before** you built it.

**All five are yours.** None are given.

An acceptance criterion names a number or something plainly observable. *"The
detector is accurate"* is an opinion — and perfect AI detection isn't possible
anyway, so it's an opinion about something nobody can deliver. *"Of 10 pieces I
wrote myself, at most 1 is labelled high-confidence AI"* is a criterion.

Under each, write a sentence or two on **why that target**. *"I allow at most 1
false positive in 10 because on a writing site a wrong accusation costs more
than a missed detection"* is a real answer.

> Missing your own targets next unit costs you nothing. Setting a target so
> easy you can't miss it does.

---

## Pick numbers you can defend

**Cover at least three of these five areas.** They're prompts, not a form.

| Area | A question it could answer |
|---|---|
| False positives | How often may a human's writing get called AI? |
| Score spread | How far apart should clearly-AI and clearly-human text score? |
| Label coverage | Is every one of your three labels actually reachable? |
| The appeal path | Does an appeal always change status and always get recorded? |
| Bad input | What happens when `text` is missing, empty, or enormous? |

Two things worth knowing before you pick numbers:

- **The expensive mistake is one-sided.** Missing some AI text costs the site a
  little. Accusing a real writer costs that person a lot. A criterion that
  treats both errors as equally bad is describing a system nobody would ship.
- **Unit 8 tests across three trials, and the target has to hold across all
  three.** Your first signal runs a model, so identical input can score
  differently twice. A target of "at most 1 in 10" against trials of 2, 1, 2 is
  a **miss**.

---
## 1. Limit false positives to one in ten

Before scoring, choose ten distinct passages you wrote yourself, including
examples of plain, everyday prose. Submit the same ten passages to
`POST /submit` in each of three trials.

Each trial passes if all ten requests return HTTP `200` with a `guess` of
`human`, `unsure`, or `ai`, and at most one guess is `ai`. A request error or
a missing or invalid `guess` fails the trial. The criterion passes only if
all three trials pass.

**Why this target?** A false AI accusation can damage a writer's credibility
and leave them defending their own work. That costs more than missing some
AI-generated writing.

---

## 2. Separate median scores by at least 0.20

Before scoring, choose ten known AI-generated passages and ten known
human-written passages with similar word counts across the two groups.
Submit the same twenty passages to `POST /submit` in each of three trials.

In each trial, require HTTP `200` and a numeric `confidence` between 0 and 1
inclusive for every passage. Subtract the median human `confidence` from
the median AI `confidence`. The gap must be at least `0.20` in each trial.

**Why this target?** A gap of `0.20` requires a meaningful difference on the
0-to-1 scale. The median limits the influence of unusually high or low scores.

---

## 3. Reach all three reader-facing labels

Choose a fixed set of passages before scoring and submit it to `POST /submit`
in each of three trials. Every request must return HTTP `200` with one of
the guesses below and a full-sentence `label` that conveys the paired meaning.
Each guess-and-label pair must appear at least once in every trial.

| `guess` | Meaning the `label` must convey |
| --- | --- |
| `human` | The text was likely written by a person. |
| `unsure` | It is unclear whether a person or AI wrote the text. |
| `ai` | The text was likely generated by AI. |

**Why this target?** Readers need to see when the service is uncertain as
well as when it leans strongly toward human or AI authorship.


---

## 4. Confirm appeals and preserve the audit trail

In each of three trials, create a fresh decision with `POST /submit`. Send
its returned `content_id` and a nonblank `reasoning` to `POST /appeal`. The
appeal must return HTTP `200`, the same `content_id`, and `status` set to
`under_review`.

In the `GET /log` entries, the original `decided` record must appear before
a record with `event` set to `appeal` and `status` set to `under_review`.
Both records must match the submission's `content_id` and `creator_id`.
The appeal record must contain the reasoning sent by the writer. A
well-formed appeal for a `content_id` with no decision must return HTTP `404`.
Every check must pass in all three trials.

**Why this target?** The confirmation tells the writer their appeal is
under review. The two audit entries let a reviewer reconstruct the decision
and see why the writer challenged it.

---

## 5. Reject invalid text and accept longer passages

In each of three trials, send separate JSON objects to `POST /submit` with
a valid `creator_id`. The service must return HTTP `400` when `text` is
omitted, `null`, a non-string value, an empty string, or a whitespace-only
string. For non-string values, try a number, a boolean, an array, and an
object. In the same trial, submit a valid passage longer than 400 characters
and require HTTP `200`. Every check must pass in all three trials.

**Why this target?** Rejecting invalid values gives callers a clear signal
to fix their request. A writer should be able to submit an ordinary passage
longer than 400 characters.
